# Bullet points

Applies to English résumés directly and to German CVs in spirit (see `german-lebenslauf.md` for
German verb style). Summarised from the r/EngineeringResumes wiki.

## What a bullet must do

1. Show the technical work done.
2. Show the technical challenge overcome.
3. Show the impact.

## Shape

- One sentence, 1–2 lines. No orphan last line holding only 1–4 words — rewrite until it either
  fits one line or fills most of the second. No hyphenated line breaks.
- Starts with a strong past-tense action verb (present tense only for the current role, if
  used consistently).
- No personal pronouns (I, we, my, our). No trailing period.
- Digits, not number words (`8`, not "eight").
- Avoid `&`, `/` and apostrophes; avoid sub-bullets.
- Cut filler adjectives and adverbs: *excellent, innovative, expert, cutting-edge, successfully,
  diligently, strategically, meticulously …*
- Order bullets best-first; many readers only see the first one or two.
- Put metrics early in the bullet when there is one.

## Formulas

Use one consistently; they are lenses, not templates to paste:

- **XYZ** — accomplished *X*, measured by *Y*, by doing *Z*.
- **CAR** — challenge → action → result.
- **STAR** — situation → task → action → result (compress S and T to a few words).

> Reduced p95 API latency 40% by replacing N+1 ORM queries with batched loaders in a
> GraphQL gateway serving 2M requests/day

## Verbs

**Prefer** (precise, plain): analyzed, architected, automated, built, created, cut, debugged,
decreased, delivered, designed, developed, implemented, improved, integrated, launched, led,
migrated, optimized, profiled, published, reduced, refactored, replaced, scaled, shipped, tested.

**Avoid — weak or passive:** aided, assisted, collaborated, communicated, contributed to,
executed, exposed to, gained experience, helped, participated, worked on, was responsible for,
ran, used, utilized, coded, programmed.

**Avoid — inflated or vague:** amplified, conceptualized, crafted, elevated, employed,
engaged, engineered, enhanced, ensured, fostered, headed, honed, innovated, leveraged, mastered,
orchestrated, perfected, pioneered, revolutionized, spearheaded, transformed.

Past tense of *lead* is *led*. Never "utilize".

## Integration over parts lists

Don't list components ("Raspberry Pi, stepper motor, React, Postgres"). Explain how the pieces
were made to work together and to what standard — interfaces, precision, throughput, trade-offs.
That is what signals engineering ability.

## Finding content (interview prompts)

Use these to interview the user in Create/Improve mode, one group at a time:

- **What:** What problem did you solve? Alone or in a team — how big? What was your part?
- **How:** Why that approach? What tools, standards or references? How did you test or validate,
  and what did testing change?
- **Result:** On time / under budget? Did it work as intended? What numbers moved (latency,
  cost, errors, users, revenue, time saved)? What went wrong and what did you learn?
- **Goal exercise:** What goals were set at the start? How were they met — and where were they
  exceeded (faster, cheaper, more capable)? Brain-dump everything first, then cut to fit.

## Honesty

Never invent metrics, scope or results. If a number would help but the user hasn't given one,
ask; if there is none, describe scope or audience instead ("for 12 internal teams"). Mark any
unconfirmed placeholder as `[?]` in drafts and list them for the user.
