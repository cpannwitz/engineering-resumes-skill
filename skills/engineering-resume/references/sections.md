# Sections (English résumé)

Section-by-section rules, summarised from the r/EngineeringResumes wiki. Points marked
**[DE differs]** are overridden by `german-lebenslauf.md` for German CVs.

## Section order

| Situation | Order |
|---|---|
| Working full-time after graduation | Experience → Skills → Education, or Skills → Experience → Education |
| Student / new grad with some work experience | Education → Experience → (Projects) → Skills |
| No *technical* work experience | Education → Projects → Experience → Skills |
| No work experience at all | Education → Projects → Volunteering / Extracurriculars → Skills |

- **Summary/profile:** only for senior/staff+ engineers, career changers, or people explaining a
  gap / return to work. Max ~2 sentences. **[DE differs: a short Kurzprofil is common]**
- **No references section**, and no "references available on request".
- Standard names only: `Experience` / `Work Experience`, `Projects`, `Education`, `Skills`.

## Contact information (header)

- Name, then one line of plain-text contact details. No labels ("Email:", "GitHub:", "Phone:").
- One email address, from a modern provider. Graduates drop the university address unless the
  school's name is a real asset.
- No street address or ZIP. City/region only when it matches the job's location (a non-local
  location can bias the screen). **[DE differs]**
- Phone and LinkedIn are optional; recruiters usually email first. If a phone is listed, no
  prefix, and omit +1 inside US/Canada. **[DE differs: phone and LinkedIn/XING are expected]**
- Links as bare text: `github.com/username`, `name.dev` — no `https://www.`, no underline,
  no colour, no hidden hyperlinks behind words like "portfolio".
- Leave out an empty or stale GitHub/portfolio.
- Work authorisation: if a name or background might suggest a need for sponsorship, a short
  status near the name ("EU citizen", "US Permanent Resident") removes doubt. Security
  clearances also go in the header.

## Experience

- Paid work only (research positions count). Label internships and contract roles as such.
- Each entry: title, employer, (location), dates; then bullets. See `bullets.md`.
- Order entries and bullets by relevance to the target role, or by impressiveness — best first.
- Tailor per application: show the job description's needs are met.
- Describe accomplishments and problems solved, not duties. A résumé is not a job description.
- Lead with the engineering skill, not the tool: "identified a capability gap and took the
  design through validation" beats "used SolidWorks". Tools are supporting detail.
- New grads: prove solid fundamentals first; leadership claims come after, if space remains.
- Confidential work: describe the technology and outcome in generic terms (standard, domain,
  metric) without naming classified programs, customers or proprietary processes.

## Projects

- Section name: `Projects` (no "Personal/Academic/Selected …" prefix); don't put the word
  "project" in titles. Title Case for titles.
- For personal projects, dates/roles/locations are usually unnecessary; a repo or demo link matters more.
- Bullets, ordered by relevance. Strong projects are real: used (even only by you), maintained,
  solving an actual problem — not tutorial clones or mandatory coursework. Design teams and
  competition teams count.
- Work projects belong under the job, not here.

## Education

- Reverse chronological. Degree, institution, graduation date only (`May 2021` or `2021`);
  current students write `Expected May 2026`. No start dates or ranges. **[DE differs]**
- No high school; no programmes left without a degree.
- *Skill addition (not in the wiki):* if **no** degree was completed, dropping all studies can
  leave a visible gap. For experienced candidates, either omit Education entirely (experience
  carries the CV) or keep one compact line for the most relevant programme, e.g.
  `Media Informatics coursework — TU Dresden, 2014 – 2018`. Never imply a degree that wasn't
  awarded. Ask the user which they prefer.
- Coursework only if unusually specialised. Awards only if exceptional (Fulbright-level).
  Competitive / D1 sports are worth listing.
- GPA: include if ≥ 3.75 (2 decimals); drop it once Education moves to the bottom after a first
  full-time job, unless outstanding. **[DE differs: Abschlussnote]**
- Omit school location if it's in the name or well known.

## Skills

- Section name: `Skills`. At most ~3 lines, single column, grouped by category
  (e.g. Languages, Frameworks, Cloud & DevOps, Tools).
- Comma-separated, nothing else (no pipes, slashes, dashes). Official capitalisation
  (`PostgreSQL`, `LabVIEW`, `CATIA V5`). Split combined items: `C, C++` not `C/C++`.
- Order by importance; question whatever sits at the end of each line.
- Include: languages you could be interviewed in; technologies you have really used. Skills
  listed here should also appear in bullets, and vice versa.
- Exclude: soft skills (show them in bullets), assumed basics (Word, typing, IDEs, OSs),
  hosting sites (`Git`, not `GitHub`), proficiency labels ("expert in …"), bolding, multiple columns.
- Early career: a few genuinely practised skills beat a long list of things only touched.
- Hobbies: not in Skills; at most a short separate line, and only if nothing stronger fits.

## Portfolios and GitHub

- Link only if current and presentable. Pinned repos need READMEs with summary, screenshots,
  how to run, how to test; runnable automated tests are a plus.

## Bias

- Leave out details that invite bias at screening: age, date of birth, gender, children,
  marital status, nationality, ethnicity, religion, political views, photo. **[DE differs: some are optional]**

## Senior engineers (10+ years)

- Optional 1–2 sentence summary; 2 pages max.
- Separate versions for management vs. individual-contributor tracks.
- Show influence as well as impact, and "soft" achievements alongside business results.
- Compress older roles; move Education to the bottom.

## Career changers

- 2-sentence summary stating the change and the motivation.
- Link working projects and source code; keep prior-career entries brief; aim for 1 page.
